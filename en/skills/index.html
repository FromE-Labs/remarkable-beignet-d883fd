Instructions you stop repeating. Judgement that does not get lost.

01 — What it is

A procedure the AI follows without you repeating it.

A Skill is a structured set of instructions, criteria and resources that an AI model loads when it recognises a task. It defines how that task must be done in your context: which inputs it expects, which steps it follows, what it checks and what it delivers.

01

What it solves

Recurring tasks that today depend on someone remembering the right instructions: reports, reviews, classification, writing to house criteria, fixed-format analysis, pre-decision checks.

02

What it improves

Consistency across people and sessions, start-up time, output quality and traceability: every run starts from the same procedure and leaves the same trail.

03

Where it fits

Any AI environment that supports persistent instructions or skill files: desktop assistants, agents, automated flows and internal tools.

04

What it is not

Not a long prompt or a text template. It is an operational design with activation conditions, validations and error behaviour.

02 — Generic versus your own

The difference is what happens when something fails.

A generic Skill describes a task. A Skill developed for your organisation describes your task, with your data, your limits and your way of validating.

Generic Skill
  • General-purpose instructions, no organisational context.
  • No explicit validation criteria: the output is assumed correct.
  • Implicit or non-existent operational limits.
  • On error, the model improvises.
  • No defined oversight: nobody knows what to review or when.
From E™ Skill for your organisation
  • Behaviour defined for your context, vocabulary and sources.
  • Explicit validations before delivery: what is checked and against what.
  • Declared operational limits: what it does not do, what it escalates to a person.
  • Error handling: detection, message, recovery path.
  • Built-in oversight: human review points and decision logging.
03 — Built-in governance

Five layers inside the same file.

Governance is not an extra layer or a separate document. It is built into the Skill's design so that behaviour stays predictable outside the ideal case too.

BehaviourHow the Skill acts: tone, format, order of operations, what it asks and what it assumes.
ValidationWhat it checks before considering the task done and by which criterion it accepts or rejects a result.
Operational limitsWhat is out of scope, which decisions it never takes alone and when it stops execution.
Error handlingWhat happens when a datum is missing, a source does not respond or the result fails validation.
OversightWhere a person steps in, what is logged and how performance is reviewed over time.
04 — Advantages

Compared with generic instructions.
